If you're paid every two weeks, you get 26 paychecks a year. Most months contain two paydays, so a monthly budget naturally ends up built around 24 of them. The other two, or sometimes three, land in the occasional months that hold three paydays.

Budgeting an extra paycheck mostly comes down to one decision made ahead of time: those checks aren't part of your regular monthly spending, and each one already has a purpose before it arrives.

## How a two-paycheck budget works

You plan every month as if it brings in exactly two paychecks. Rent, bills, groceries, debt minimums and any regular savings all have to fit inside that amount. When a month brings a third paycheck, the budget doesn't change, and the third check goes wherever you decided it would go.

This only works if your regular expenses already fit inside two paychecks. Many people's don't at first, especially if a third paycheck has been quietly filling the gap. If that's you, getting the monthly budget to balance on two paychecks is the first step, and the [biweekly budget guide](/articles/budget-biweekly-paycheck/) walks through assigning each bill to the paycheck that arrives before it's due.

Two-paycheck months can also feel tight compared with your yearly salary divided by 12. A small cash buffer in checking makes those months easier, and building one is often the first job for an extra paycheck.

## How much the extra paychecks add up to

Take a take-home paycheck of $2,150, paid every other Friday.

| | Paychecks | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Paid in a 26-paycheck year | 26 × $2,150 | $55,900 |
| Covered by a two-paycheck monthly budget | 24 × $2,150 | $51,600 |
| Not committed to monthly spending | 2 × $2,150 | $4,300 |

In a year with 27 paydays, which happens on some schedules in 2027, the same budget leaves three paychecks uncommitted: 27 × $2,150 is $58,050, and $6,450 of it arrives outside the monthly plan.

This isn't extra income. It's the same salary. Building regular spending around 24 paychecks means the remaining two or three arrive with nothing already depending on them, which makes them much easier to put toward something deliberate.

## Three-paycheck months in 2027

Which months have three paydays depends on your schedule. Every-other-Friday pay follows one of two alternating cycles:

| Regular Friday payday cycle | Three-paycheck months | Scheduled paydays in 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| January 1 cycle | January, July, December | 27 |
| January 8 cycle | April, October | 26 |

The January 1 cycle comes with a complication. January 1 is a federal holiday, and some employers pay a holiday payday on the last business day before it, which here is Thursday, December 31, 2026. Others pay on the next business day, and practice varies by employer. If yours pays on December 31, that check belongs to 2026: January 2027 then has two paydays (the 15th and 29th), July (2, 16, 30) and December (3, 17, 31) still have three, and you receive 26 paychecks in 2027 instead of 27.

What counts is the date the money is deposited. Your pay stub or your employer's payroll calendar will show it, or you can enter any recent payday in the calculator below to see every payday for 2026 through 2028.

## Deciding what each extra paycheck is for

The [biweekly budget guide](/articles/budget-biweekly-paycheck/) lists the usual options: a cash buffer, an extra debt payment, an upcoming irregular expense, or a savings goal. The harder part is choosing between them, and the right order depends on where you are now.

If you have little or no emergency savings, start there. Without a cushion, the next car repair or short month tends to end up on a credit card, which undoes whatever progress the extra paycheck made elsewhere. Even one paycheck set aside changes how a bad month plays out.

Once you have a basic cushion, high-interest debt is often a good next priority. Credit card interest is usually far higher than what a savings account earns, so paying down a balance often does more for you than saving the same amount.

Your own situation can change that order. An overdue essential bill comes before any of this. If your employer matches retirement contributions and you aren't contributing enough to get the full match, that may be worth fixing first, though it usually happens through payroll rather than from a spare paycheck. And a large expense you already know is coming before your next extra paycheck, like an annual insurance premium, a tuition bill or holiday travel, can reasonably move ahead, because paying it from a paycheck you planned for keeps it off a card. [Sinking funds](/articles/sinking-funds-explained/) are a simple way to set money aside for these.

If you have a cushion and no high-interest debt, the extra paychecks are free for bigger goals: a fuller emergency fund, a down payment, retirement savings or a large planned purchase.

Whatever order you choose, decide before the money arrives and write the decision down where you'll see it. It also helps to move the money on the day it's deposited, before it sits in checking long enough to blend in with everything else.

## A sample year

Here's how the January 1 cycle might look for someone with a small cash buffer and a credit card balance, using the $2,150 example:

| Month | Extra paycheck | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| January | $2,150 | Build the emergency buffer |
| July | $2,150 | Pay down the credit card |
| December | $2,150 | Pay down the credit card, or fund next year's annual bills |

If this person's employer paid the January 1 check on December 31, 2026, the January row disappears and the plan covers July and December.

## Planning ahead each year

The dates change every year, but the approach doesn't: keep your monthly budget on two paychecks, look up which months will bring a third, and decide what each of those paychecks is for while it's still months away. If you track spending in a spreadsheet, the [Budget Spreadsheet](/spreadsheets/budget-spreadsheet/) lets you set your own budget period with a start and end date, and a [savings goals tracker](/spreadsheets/savings-goals-tracker/) gives each extra paycheck a target and shows its progress as you log deposits.
